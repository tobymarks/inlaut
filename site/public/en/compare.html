Dictation apps for the Mac compared

Local or cloud, free or subscription, plain dictation or AI post-processing: this overview puts five dictation apps for the Mac side by side. Everything said about the other apps comes from the vendors' own pages.

As of 10 October 2026. This page is written by the developer of inlaut, one of the five apps.

In short

  • Apple Dictation is part of macOS and needs no installation.
  • Wispr Flow post-processes your text with AI and also runs on Windows, iPhone and Android. Speech recognition happens in the cloud.
  • Superwhisper lets you choose between local and cloud models and offers AI modes in its Pro version.
  • MacWhisper is mainly a tool for transcribing files, videos and meetings; the direct version can also dictate system-wide.
  • inlaut does one thing: dictation into any app, entirely on the Mac, free, open source and without an account. It speaks English and German.

What this page does not compare

Recognition quality. It depends on your voice, accent, language, microphone and the model you choose, and we don't have a fair test of all five apps under the same conditions. All five can be tried for free. Your own trial with the kind of text you write tells you the most.

Which app fits when

You don't want to install anything: macOS dictation is already there. Keyboard settings show whether it works locally on your Mac.

You want the text polished right away, or you also dictate on your phone and on Windows: Wispr Flow and Superwhisper offer AI post-processing and run on several platforms.

You want to transcribe recordings, videos or meetings: that is MacWhisper's focus; Superwhisper (Pro) transcribes files too.

You simply want to dictate, in English or German, without an account and without speech leaving your Mac: that is what inlaut is built for. It deliberately does less than the others: no AI post-processing, no file transcription, and only Macs with Apple silicon and macOS 26.
